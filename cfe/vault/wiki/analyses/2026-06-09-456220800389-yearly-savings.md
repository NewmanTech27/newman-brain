**Recommendation: PV 700 kWp + BESS 1,700 kWh / 850 kW.** Because the umbral never binds,
the two levers stack almost additively (PV $2.53M + BESS $1.86M ≈ combined $4.31M gross).
- **PV is the dominant lever** and the highest-ROI component (PV-only: TIR ~31.5%, payback
  ~3.5 yr, NPV ~$17.0M on $9.21M CAPEX). PV lands in *intermedio* under SIN (solar ends
  before the 18:00–22:00 punta), so it cuts energy charges, not punta demand.
- **BESS adds the capacidad shave** on the ~850 kW punta peak. BESS was the trade-off
  variable: a full-coverage 3,397 kWh battery shaved more demand but its CAPEX ($20.4M
  on the BESS alone) crushed returns. The **1,700 kWh / 850 kW** point captures the
  high-value 2h summer shave and partial 4h winter shave at the best NPV/IRR (plateau
  optimum across a 1,600–1,800 kWh scan).

**The bigger battery is strictly worse here — more CAPEX *and* less total savings.** It
does shave more demand (capacidad savings rise $3.28M → $4.21M; it fully covers the 4h
winter punta — Dec residual punta drops from 409 kW to ~10 kW), but in SIN the
punta→base price spread is unfavorable, so doubling throughput roughly doubles the
**negative arbitrage** (−$1.42M → −$2.84M) and the round-trip losses, which more than
eats the extra capacidad gain. Sizing past the ~850 kW punta peak buys no further
demand reduction while still paying to charge. The trade-off is *not* "more savings vs
lower IRR" — it is **lower IRR for lower savings**. The 1,700 kWh point dominates.
(If the punta tariff spread or the demand structure changed, the full-coverage battery
could win on absolute savings; it does not under this year's SIN rates.)

### 4a. Power-factor correction — quantified line item

The site runs **FP 85.7–87.5% every month**, below CFE's 90% floor, so each bill carries a
*cargo por bajo factor de potencia*. Summing that line across the 12 bills (it sits between
the MEM importes subtotal and IVA in the CFDI):

| | Annual |
|---|---|
| **FP penalty (sin IVA), measured from the 12 bills** | **$874,974** |
| FP penalty incl. IVA 16% | $1,014,970 |
| Range per bill | $61,391 (Feb) – $98,294 (Apr) |

This penalty is **not** in the $15,620,783 baseline (the baseline is the MEM-importes sum;
the FP charge is a separate line), so correcting it is **genuine additive savings, not
double-counted**. Correcting FP to ≥90% drives the penalty to ~$0 (and to a small *bonif*
if pushed to ≥95%, conservatively modeled here as $0). Two correction paths:

1. **Via the hybrid/BESS inverter** (reactive/VAR support) — effectively **zero incremental
   CAPEX**. This is the assumed path in the "WITH FP" column above: it adds a flat
   ~$874,974/yr recurring saving (escalating with CFE, no degradation, no availability
   haircut), lifting net Año-1 to **$4,448,455**, project **TIR 22.5% → 27.8%**, payback
   **5.0 → 4.0 yr**, NPV **$18.1M → $28.5M**.
2. **Standalone automatic capacitor bank** (cleaner if BESS slips or for immediate value):
   ~**300 kVAr** detuned/stepped bank to lift peak FP (~967 kW load) from 0.857 to ~0.95.
   Rough installed CAPEX **~$180k–$300k** (600–1,000 MXN/kVAr). **Standalone payback ~3–4
   months** against the $874,974/yr penalty — the single highest-ROI intervention on the
   site, and worth doing **now** regardless of the PV/BESS decision.

## 5. Findings & risks
- **Power-factor penalty every month — now quantified (§4a):** FP ranges 85.7–87.5%, below
  the 90% threshold, so every bill carries a *cargo por bajo factor de potencia*. Summed
  across the 12 bills the penalty is **$874,974/yr (sin IVA; ~$1.01M incl. IVA)** —
  $61k–$98k/month. It is **not** in the engine baseline (separate bill line), so correcting
  FP to ≥90% (via the hybrid/BESS inverter at ~$0 incremental CAPEX, or a ~300 kVAr
  capacitor bank at ~$180k–$300k with ~3–4 month standalone payback) is **additive
  savings**. Folded into the recommended config it lifts net Año-1 to $4,448,455, TIR to
  27.8%, payback to 4.0 yr (§4). This is the **highest-ROI lever on the site** and should
  proceed regardless of the PV/BESS build.
- **PV is capped by the < 0.7 MW DG ceiling**, not by roof area (area not provided). 700
  kWp keeps the site a [[generador-exento]] under [[generacion-distribuida]] (art. 25 Ley
  2025) eligible for [[medicion-neta]]. At 0.17 kWp/m² this implies ~4,120 m² of usable
  area — **confirm the site has it**. If area < ~4,120 m², PV (and total savings) shrink.
- **Demand-bound, umbral never binds** → PV+BESS savings are additive here; no umbral
  neutralization risk ([[pv-bess-combined]]).
- PV self-consumption is only ~23% of annual load (1.28 GWh of 5.58 GWh), so essentially
  all generation is consumed on-site — minimal net export, clean medición-neta fit.

## 6. Engine change — CFDI XML loader
These bills are **CFDI XML** (SAT cfd/4 + iTComplements CFE complemento), not PDF. The
engine's `extract.py` only handled PDF. Added `parse_bill_xml()` + `_xml_text_map()` and
made `extract_folder()` auto-detect `*.xml` alongside `*.pdf`. The CFE bill data lives in a
flat `clsRegArchFact` block: MEM importes by concept code (ES1→suministro, ED1→distribución,
ETB→transmisión, ECB→CENACE, EGB/EGI/EGP→generación B/I/P, EID→capacidad, EMB→SCnMEM),
kWh by period (CONSUMO1F/2F/3F = base/inter/punta), demands (DEMANDA1P/2P/3P). Raw bytes
never enter LLM context (parsed locally). The PDF golden test (RPU 780881200029) still
reproduces to the peso — the change is additive.
