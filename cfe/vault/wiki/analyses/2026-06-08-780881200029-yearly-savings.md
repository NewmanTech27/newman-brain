# Yearly PV+BESS Savings — Service 780881200029 (Grupo Posadas Cancún)

**Question:** From 12 months of real GDMTH bills, what is the annual PV+BESS savings for this site — and how do my numbers compare to the client's manual Excel model? Which configuration (PV-only / BESS-only / combined) is best?

> **Mode:** Diff-and-explain. I change nothing in the Excel; I reproduce the baseline independently, sanity-bound the savings, and flag where the Excel is optimistic or conservative.

---

## 1. Baseline — VALIDATED, high confidence

I extracted all 12 PDFs independently (pdfplumber) and reconciled every field against the Excel's `TARIFF_RATES`/`BASELINE_COST`:

- **Every consumption and MEM cost field matches to the peso** across all 12 bills (kWh B/I/P, kW B/I/P, Suministro, Distribución, Transmisión, CENACE, Generación B/I/P, Capacidad, SCnMEM).
- The only field that differs is the informational `KWMax` register (ABR/OCT/DIC) — and the Excel correctly used `max(B,I,P)` for billing (see [[demanda-facturable]] correction).

**Annual baseline (sin IVA): $30,157,371** — agreed. Composition:

| Component | Annual $ | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Energy (base+inter+punta, incl. flat T&D/CENACE/SCnMEM) | ~20.65M | ~68% |
| Cargo por Capacidad (on kW punta) | 7,570,382 | 25.1% |
| Cargo por Distribución (on max(B,I,P)) | 1,934,354 | 6.4% |
| Cargo fijo | 5,152 | <0.1% |

**Two structural facts that drive everything below:**
1. **The umbral never binds** — measured punta demand (1,371–1,607 kW) is below the umbral/piso (1,819–2,368 kW) every month. So every kW of peak shaving converts **directly** to capacidad savings; there is no umbral floor fighting the BESS, and PV lowering the umbral has **no** demand effect. (The dangerous PV-neutralizes-BESS interaction in [[pv-bess-combined]] does **not** apply here.)
2. **Solar makes ~zero kWh in punta** (SIN punta 18–22 winter / 20–22 summer). PV savings are pure intermedio energy offset; PV does nothing for demand charges.

## 2. Savings — my independent bound vs. the Excel (corrected after reviewing DISPATCH)

> **Correction (2026-06-08, same day):** An earlier version of this page claimed the gross savings were "optimistic" because the winter energy limit wasn't modeled. **That was wrong.** The `DISPATCH` sheet *does* enforce it — see the monthly "Pico post-BESS" column below. And the 0.75 "Factor BESS falla" is **not** a stand-in for the winter limit; it is a separate **business availability assumption** (savings-as-a-service: the financier assumes the system is down ~3 months/yr). The two are independent and both legitimate.

> **Correction (2026-06-11, full formula audit — see [[2026-06-11-780881200029-calculadora-audit]]):** Two findings supersede parts of this page. (1) **The arbitrage "✅ agree" below was under-scrutinized:** the Excel cycles the battery 365 days/yr with no cap against the bill's punta energy — punta only exists on weekdays, and e.g. April credits 82,961 punta kWh against a bill holding 40,790. Corrected arbitrage: **$790,049/yr, not $1,216,238** (−35%). (2) **The +1.2% baseline overshoot is solved peso-exact:** it is the FP **bonificación** (site FP 99.5–100%) that the Excel never transcribed — −$356,110/yr; real baseline $29,801,261, and a ~$85k/yr claw-back applies to savings. **Corrected gross: $7,083,252 (23.5%) vs the $7,593,969 (25.2%) below.** The engine shares the arbitrage method (golden-tested) — fix pending, flagged in [[overview]] §Open gaps.

The dispatch shaves the **measured** punta peak (anchored to the real bill `kW punta`), energy-limited per season:

| Season | Usable ÷ punta hours | Modeled BESS shave | Post-BESS residual peak (DISPATCH) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summer (7 mo, 2h punta) | 2,822 ÷ 2 = **1,411 kW** | ~full peak | **0–224 kW** |
| Winter (5 mo, 4h punta) | 2,822 ÷ 4 = **705 kW** | partial | **753–892 kW** |

| Metric | Excel GROSS | Excel NET (×0.75 availability) | My independent estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| BESS demand (capacidad) | $5,590,209 | — | **~$5.3–5.6M** ✅ agree |
| BESS arbitrage (base→punta) | $1,216,238 | — | **~$1.0–1.2M** ✅ agree |
| PV (client PPA savings) | $787,522 | — | **~$0.8M** ✅ agree |
| **Total Year-1 savings** | **$7,593,969 (25.2%)** | **$5.78M (19.2%)** | **~$7.0–7.6M gross** ✅ converges |

**Conclusion on the diff:**
- ✅ **Baseline:** exact agreement.
- ✅ **Gross savings ($7.59M / 25.2%) are energy-consistent and defensible.** Once I use the correct hour-divided shave and add the base→punta arbitrage I had under-weighted, my independent numbers converge on the Excel's. The flat-floor shave (hold the reduction across the whole punta window) is the correct *conservative* way to guarantee the billed-peak reduction.
- ✅ **Net ($5.78M / 19.2%) is a conservative business case** — gross minus a ~3-month/yr downtime assumption for the savings-as-a-service guarantee.

Demand + arbitrage are **not** double-counted: the punta-discharged kWh both lower the $/kW peak (capacidad) and displace energy that was charged at the base rate ($/kWh) — two separate bill line items.

## 3. Which configuration is best?

| Config | Annual savings | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **BESS-only** | ~$5.0–5.5M | Captures the dominant lever (demand charges + arbitrage). The whole value is here. |
| **PV-only** | ~$0.8–1.0M | Pure intermedio energy offset; zero demand benefit (SIN punta). Always self-consumed (3.4% of load) → no credit-expiry risk. |
| **PV + BESS combined** | ~$5.8–6.5M | **Essentially additive** — because the umbral doesn't bind and PV doesn't touch punta demand, there is no interaction penalty. Best absolute savings. |

**Recommendation:** **Combined is best in absolute terms and the savings are additive here** (no umbral conflict). But **BESS is ~85% of the value and should be prioritized** — if capital is constrained, BESS-only captures most of the benefit. PV is a low-risk energy adder with **significant scale-up headroom**: it's sized at only 194 kWp on a 5,115 kW site, and since generation (350 MWh) is always below consumption (10.4 GWh) there is never net export — so a larger array keeps offsetting intermedio energy at ~$2/kWh with no medición-neta credit-expiry risk. Worth modeling a 500–700 kW PV case (still under the new <0.7 MW DG ceiling — see [[generacion-distribuida]]).

## 4. Findings filed back to the wiki

1. **Distribución billing base** — CFE bills distribución on `max(kW B, I, P)`, **not** the printed `KWMax`. Verified on ABR/OCT/DIC. Corrected [[demanda-facturable]] and [[gdmth-bill-structure]].
2. **DG ceiling is < 0.7 MW** (art. 25 Ley del Sector Eléctrico, via [[2026-06-08-pdse-2025-2039]]), up from 0.5 MW. Relevant to PV sizing headroom.
3. **Umbral never binds for this load shape** — simplifies the PV+BESS model to additive.

## 5. Risks / open items for the Excel (the remaining ones)

- **⚠ Intra-day load shape is modeled, not metered — this is the biggest residual risk.** `CONSUMPTION_PROFILE` scales an *industry* hourly curve to match the bill's peak demand; its own validation cell shows the modeled daily kWh runs **~26% above** the CFE average (peak matches, energy distribution doesn't). The monthly aggregation is anchored to real kWh and real peaks, so totals are sound — but the *feasibility of the peak shave* and the *demand-vs-arbitrage split* depend on the true within-punta shape. **Pull actual 15-min HM interval data for a representative summer and winter day and re-run dispatch** — this is what turns a modeled proposal into a bankable, guarantee-able one. (Note: real punta load is peaky — avg punta kW ≈ punta_kWh/(d×punta_h) is well below the peak — which generally means *less* energy is needed to shave the peak, freeing more for arbitrage. Likely favorable, but verify.)
- **0.75 availability is applied flat (season-blind).** For a savings *guarantee* where you absorb downtime, a failed *summer* month (~$600k) costs ~2× a failed *winter* month (~$280k). A flat 0.75 implicitly assumes average-value months fail; a prudent stress case assumes the **3 highest-value (summer) months** fail. Worth a seasonally-weighted downside scenario.
- **Degradation vs. demand savings in later years.** BESS degrades 1.25%/yr → usable drops ~17% by yr 15 → the hour-divided shave shrinks (winter 705→~585 kW). Confirm the projection re-derives the *demand* shave from degraded usable kWh each year, not just the energy throughput.
- ~~**+1.2% systematic reconstruction overshoot** in `BASELINE_COST` (calc > receipt, constant) — likely a small unmodeled bonificación/credit. Immaterial but worth identifying.~~ **SOLVED 2026-06-11:** it is the FP bonificación (FP 99.5–100% every month), peso-exact — see [[2026-06-11-780881200029-calculadora-audit]].
- **20-yr projection sensitivity:** 6%/yr CFE vs 5%/yr PPA escalation drives the VPN; small changes compound.
