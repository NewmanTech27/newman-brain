# IEA-PVPS T13-32:2025 — Optimisation of PV Systems for Different Climates

**Type:** research report (IEA PVPS Task 13)
**Date:** June 2025
**Author:** IEA PVPS Task 13 (Friesen, Micheli, Eder, et al.)
**Raw file:** `raw/pdfs/2025-07-02 - IEA-PVPS-T13-32-2025-REPORT-Climate-Optimisation-2025.pdf`

## Summary
How to choose and design PV systems for the climate they operate in. Introduces
**Climate-Specific Energy Rating (CSER)** and **climate-specific Performance Loss Rates
(PLR)**, then gives stressors + mitigation for three climate families: **cold & snowy**,
**hot & dry**, and **hot & humid**. The **hot & humid** chapter is directly on-point for our
core market (Cancún, Yucatán, Ciudad del Carmen) — it informs module technology selection,
expected degradation, and O&M (cleaning/soiling) for sites like Grupo Posadas
([[2026-06-08-780881200029-yearly-savings]]).

## Key claims
- **Module energy yield and PLR are climate-specific** — a single generic derate misrepresents real performance; rate by climate (CSER).
- **Hot & humid** stressors: high temperature (thermal derate), humidity-driven **encapsulation degradation / corrosion / PID**, soiling — favors **low temp-coefficient and moisture-robust** modules.
- Case studies benchmark module technologies per climate → supports HJT/low-tempco selection for hot-humid coasts (cf. Seraphim in [[pv-modules]]).
- Bifacial systems behave differently by climate/albedo (relevant to our bifacial stock).
