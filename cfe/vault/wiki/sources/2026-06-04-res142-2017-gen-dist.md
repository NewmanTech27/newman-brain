# DACG Generación Distribuida — RES/142/2017

**Type:** administrative regulation (DACG + contract models + compensation methodology)  
**Date:** Issued February 16, 2017; Published DOF March 7, 2017  
**Author:** [[cre]] — Comisión Reguladora de Energía  
**Raw file:** `raw/pdfs/2025-07-02 - (DOF 2017-03-07 CRE) RES-142-2017 DACG Generación Distribuida.pdf`

## Summary

CRE's foundational regulation governing **distributed generation (DG) ≤ 0.5 MW** interconnected to the Redes Generales de Distribución (RGD). Covers: access rules, three compensation models, interconnection contract templates, technical specifications for metering, and interconnection study requirements.

This regulation is directly relevant to [[gdmth]] users who install solar panels (or other DG ≤ 0.5 MW) on their premises. It defines how the energy they generate is credited against their GDMTH bill, and which compensation model they can use.

**Note:** Mexico had 151 MW of distributed generation at the time of this regulation (0.22% of installed capacity). This rule was designed for the country's first stage of DG integration. As of 2025, this may have been reviewed or modified — see [[2026-06-04-ley-sector-electrico-2025]].

## Three compensation models

Users who sign an interconnection contract become **Generadores Exentos** (exempt generators). They choose one of three compensation models:

### 1. Medición Neta de Energía (Net Metering) — Default
- CFE-supplied energy and user-generated energy are **netted** within the billing period
- Net deficit → billed at normal tariff rates by period (punta/intermedio/base)
- Net surplus (more generated than consumed) → becomes an **energy credit** for up to 12 months, classified by period and month of generation
- After 12 months without full compensation → liquidated at the **Precio Marginal Local (PML)** average for the interval when the credit was generated
- Payment via bank transfer

**Key rule for GDMTH:** Credits from different periods are applied in a strict priority order: punta-to-punta first, then punta-to-intermedio, then punta-to-base, then intermedio-to-punta, etc. (full matrix defined). Energy from different months is converted to equivalent kWh using the ratio of rates from the credit's origin month to the billing month.

### 2. Facturación Neta (Net Billing) — Optional
- Energy exported to the grid is valued and paid **independently** from energy consumed from the grid
- Export valued at the PML (Local Marginal Price) at the hour of delivery, at the specific interconnection node
- More complex but potentially better for users with low self-consumption
- Payment within 30 days after invoice

### 3. Venta Total de Energía — Optional
- All generated energy sold to CFE SSB at PML
- User buys all consumption from grid at normal tariff rates
- Only viable when there is no associated supply contract at the same interconnection point
- Also valued at hourly PML at the interconnection node

## Interconnection process

- **No interconnection study required** in most cases ≤ 0.5 MW in typical configurations
- Study required only if: new interconnection point unrelated to existing supply contract; different phase count; or aggregate DG capacity exceeds 80% of the distribution transformer or circuit capacity (medium tension)
- Interconnection contract between user (Solicitante) and CFE Distribución (Distribuidor)
- Compensation contract between user (Generador Exento) and [[cfe-ssb]]
- Migration between compensation models: allowed only after 1 year; cannot return to old contract model once migrated

## Technical specifications (meter requirements)

For medium-tension connections (relevant for GDMTH users):
- Bidirectional fiscal meter per CFE spec G0000-48
- 0.2% accuracy class; kWh + kVARh measurement (inductive and capacitive)
- 15-minute demand interval capability; 4 programmable time-of-use periods
- Memory: ≥ 35 days at 5-minute intervals for ≥ 2 variables
- Remote communication capability (telephone modem ≥ 1200 baud)
- Battery backup ≥ 5 years

## Contradictions / tensions

- Issued in 2017 under the LIE (2014) framework. The [[2026-06-04-ley-sector-electrico-2025|2025 Ley del Sector Eléctrico]] may require reinterpretation. The regulation explicitly states it would be reviewed "after one year or when DG reaches 5% of installed capacity" — Mexico's DG has grown significantly since 2017 (from 151 MW).
- The net metering credit liquidation at PML (after 12 months) may be very unfavorable if PML is low in the region/hour — users should be aware this is not a guaranteed retail-rate credit.
