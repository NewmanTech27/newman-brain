# DACG Sistemas de Almacenamiento — Acuerdo A/113/2024

**Type:** administrative regulation (DACG)  
**Date:** Issued September 30, 2024; Published DOF March 7, 2025  
**Author:** [[cre]] — Comisión Reguladora de Energía  
**Raw file:** `raw/pdfs/2025-04-09 - 2025_03_07_DACG Sistemas de almacenamiento energia electrica CRE.pdf`

## Summary

CRE's foundational regulation for integrating Energy Storage Systems (Sistemas de Almacenamiento de Energía Eléctrica — SAE) into Mexico's national electricity system. Issued as Acuerdo A/113/2024, it defines four integration modalities, the regulatory treatment of each, interconnection requirements, market participation rules, and transitional provisions.

This regulation fills a gap left by prior rules (Bases del Mercado, 2015) which only handled storage under the "Central Eléctrica" framework. It explicitly addresses commercial and industrial users who want to install batteries behind-the-meter (the SAE-CC modality), the most relevant case for [[gdmth]] users.

## SAE Modalities

| Modality | Description | Grid injection? | Permit required? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SAE-CE** | Battery associated with an intermittent generation plant (solar/wind), sharing the same interconnection point | Yes (from CE) | Yes (or modify existing) |
| **SAE-CC** | Battery associated with a load center — no generation plant | No | No (under SSB) or No (under MEM as qualified user) |
| **SAE-AA** | Battery associated with an isolated supply (autoabastecimiento) scheme | Isolated | Yes |
| **SAE no Asociado** | Standalone battery connected independently to RNT/RGD | Yes | Yes |

## SAE-CC — Key rules for GDMTH users

This is the most relevant modality for commercial/industrial users on the [[gdmth]] tariff:

- No grid injection allowed — energy stored is used exclusively to serve the load center's own demand
- Battery power (Potencia SAE) forms part of the user's **contracted demand or maximum demand** — cannot exceed contracted demand
- If maximum demand ever exceeds contracted demand in a billing period, CENACE or the Distribuidor will request connection studies
- Users connected at media or alta tensión who install a SAE-CC must notify CRE within **90 business days** of installation, providing: technical specs, location, single-line diagram, connection point, description confirming no grid injection
- Under CFE SSB (Suministro Básico): **no generation permit required**
- CRE publishes quarterly statistics on SAE-CC installations

**Billing implication:** Because battery discharge reduces grid consumption during peak hours, the SAE-CC can lower measured peak demand (Dmax_punta or Dmax_mensual), thereby reducing the capacity and distribution charges under [[demanda-facturable]].

## SAE-CE — Battery + solar rules

- SAE and solar plant share one interconnection point
- Battery charges primarily from the solar plant; charging from grid requires separate connection studies
- CENACE can require or request battery charging/discharging for grid stability reasons
- Battery storage capacity does not earn additional CEL certificates

## Market participation

- SAE-CE and SAE no Asociado can offer ancillary services in the MEM (frequency reserves, spinning reserves, etc.), pending CENACE rule updates within 270 days of the regulation's entry into force
- SAE-CC under MEM: participates as a qualified user load center; cannot earn revenue from selling energy

## Transitional provisions (deadlines from March 2025 entry into force)

| Deadline | Action |
|---|---|
| 180 days | CRE updates interconnection/connection contract templates to include SAE provisions |
| 270 days | CRE issues regulated tariff for Non-MEM ancillary services |
| 270 days | CENACE issues operational guide for SAE market optimization models |
| 270 days | CENACE updates Power Balance Market manual for SAE |
| 1 year | CENACE updates SIASIC (interconnection system) for SAE studies |
| 1 year | CENACE adapts MEM mechanisms to accept SAE offers |

## Contradictions / tensions

- This regulation was issued under the LIE (2014) framework. As of March 2025, the [[2026-06-04-ley-sector-electrico-2025|new Ley del Sector Eléctrico]] was published the same month. The regulation references LIE articles — the extent to which it remains valid under the new law needs monitoring.
- The regulation's SAE-CC rules (Potencia SAE counts as contracted demand) interact with the GDMTH contracted demand rules in [[gdmth]] — a user adding a large battery may need to revise their contracted demand upward.
