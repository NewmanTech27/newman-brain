# Ley del Sector Eléctrico — DOF 18 marzo 2025

**Type:** legislation (decreto presidencial)  
**Date:** March 18, 2025  
**Author:** Congreso General — promulgated by Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo  
**Raw file:** `raw/pdfs/2025-07-02 - Ley del sector electrico DOF 18032025-VES.pdf`

## Summary

A landmark legislative decree published on March 18, 2025 that fundamentally restructures Mexico's electricity sector. The decree simultaneously enacts seven new laws:

1. **Ley de la Empresa Pública del Estado, CFE** — reorganizes CFE from "empresa productiva del Estado" to "empresa pública del Estado"; redefines governance, mission, and organizational structure
2. **Ley de la Empresa Pública del Estado, Petróleos Mexicanos** — equivalent for PEMEX
3. **Ley del Sector Eléctrico** — the new regulatory framework for the electricity sector (replaces or substantially modifies the Ley de la Industria Eléctrica, LIE 2014)
4. **Ley del Sector Hidrocarburos**
5. **Ley de Planeación y Transición Energética**
6. **Ley de Biocombustibles**
7. **Ley de Geotermia**
8. **Ley de la Comisión Nacional de Energía** — formally creates [[cne]] as a standalone statutory body

Also reforms the Ley del Fondo Mexicano del Petróleo and the Ley Orgánica de la Administración Pública Federal.

**The 270-page PDF is heavily focused on the CFE governance restructuring (Títulos I–IV of the Ley de la Empresa Pública del Estado, CFE).** The Ley del Sector Eléctrico itself starts later in the document; the pages ingested cover primarily CFE's new organizational structure.

## Key claims — Ley de la Empresa Pública del Estado, CFE

- CFE is now an "empresa pública del Estado" — key distinction from prior "empresa productiva del Estado" status under LIE 2014
- CFE's stated object: "procurar la justicia energética para el pueblo de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos y el desarrollo sustentable" — framing has shifted to social justice as primary mandate
- CFE provides electricity service at the lowest possible price and contributes to Internet/telecommunications provision
- CFE activities: generation, storage, transmission, distribution, commercialization, supply, plus Internet/telecom
- CFE can operate through subsidiary companies ("empresas filiales") and "esquemas de desarrollo mixto" with private sector
- Consejo de Administración: 8 members — Energy Secretary (chairs, voto de calidad), SHCP, SEMARNAT, SENACYT, PEMEX DG, 2 independent (appointed by President, ratified by Senate), 1 workers' representative
- Independent board members: 5-year staggered terms, can be renewed once; appointed by Executive, ratified by 2/3 Senate vote within 30 days
- CFE can set its own prices (via special committee with SHCP + SENER representatives), "except those determined by applicable laws" — tariff regulation remains under CRE/CNE

## Key claims — Structural significance for this wiki

- The old LIE (2014) framework under which all prior CFE tariff regulations were issued (including GDMTH rules, SAE/A113/2024, gen dist/RES-142/2017) is now superseded or must be reinterpreted under the new Ley del Sector Eléctrico
- [[cenace]] continues to exist and operate the wholesale electricity market
- [[cre]] (Comisión Reguladora de Energía) continues as the sector regulator
- [[cne]] (Comisión Nacional de Energía) is now formalized as a statutory body with its own law — previously it was a less-defined entity

- Energy storage explicitly included in CFE's object ("almacenamiento")

## Contradictions / tensions

- The 2025 law creates a tension with regulations issued under the LIE 2014 framework (such as [[2026-06-04-res142-2017-gen-dist]] and [[2026-06-04-dacg-sae-a113-2024]]). Those regulations reference LIE articles that may be superseded. The extent to which they remain valid requires monitoring — typically pre-existing CRE resolutions remain in force until explicitly replaced.
- CFE's "empresa pública" status vs. prior "empresa productiva" status may affect how CFE's commercial activities and tariff obligations are interpreted by courts.
